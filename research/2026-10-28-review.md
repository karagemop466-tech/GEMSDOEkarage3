## Why GEMSDOE32 was reportedly best — what is and is not established

The reported historical value **0.2778 is USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED**, not
ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED: no hash-bound organizer submission-page receipt is present.
The prompt's competing leaderboard snapshots (0.3195 and 0.4413) cannot both be
called a verified current observation. We do not label either current.

The reproduced geometry establishes that GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have identical
in-footprint binary supports despite distinct file encodings; GEMSDOE31 is a
subset; GEMSDOE32 equals GEMSDOE31 with dots within two pixels of the catalogue
removed (2,545 removals). GEMSDOE36 adds 1,200 dots to GEMSDOE32. This is evidence
of lineage, not of independent geological discoveries.

The max-distance-weighted recall interpretation makes a sparse, well-spaced
support potentially more efficient than redundant ribbons: extra nearby pixels
may incur false-positive cost without a corresponding recall gain. Excluding
known catalogue neighborhoods may also help if hidden new-fault benefit is small
there. But the hidden truth is unavailable: none of these geometric facts proves
why a particular hidden-test score changed, that an exclusion collar always
helps, or that our next TIFF will exceed the reported value. The previous README's
claim that any dot saturates an entire 300 m disk was misleading: a triangular
kernel decays continuously and does not confer unit credit throughout the disk.

The earlier [review](2026-10-27-review.md) gives the removal inequality and the
catalogue-censoring problem. A historical final raster constructed to avoid all
catalogue pixels cannot serve as an unbiased hide-and-recover control against
those same catalogue segments. The *rule* must be reconstructed fold-locally from
visible faults, including any emission collar and proximity feature.
