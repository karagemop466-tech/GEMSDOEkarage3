1 · Thermal-residual / conductive-boundary coincidence INGENIOUS 2 m temperature probes; competition conductivity, depth-to-conductive-base, and optionally strain/dilatation. Test spatially coherent thermal residuals along independent conductive/subsurface boundaries; use leave-area-out interpolation. Permeable faults can focus warm groundwater. A thermal anomaly aligned with an independent subsurface discontinuity is a candidate hidden conduit, not a mapped fault label. GBCGE describes 2 m surveys as useful where surface indicators are absent; the USGS integrated blind-system study supports cross-domain corroboration, not predictive performance. Solar/shade or seasonal effects, shallow groundwater, soil moisture/texture, elevation, sampling bias, interpolation artifacts. New to this checkout; unlike a terrain-only scarp score. Prior participant pages mention thermal/geothermal proxies, so cross-site novelty is unproven. Qualitative upside: highest of these, conditional. Cost: high. No numeric uplift projected. Public GDR 1391 lists the 2 m temperature-probe data. Exact coordinate coverage, attributes, license, and fit to the contest footprint still require GIS/metadata checks.
2 · Drainage-profile deflection / paired knickzones USGS 3DEP 1 m bare-earth DEM where available, plus GeoDAWN magnetic/gravity boundaries or strain. Detect repeated channel-profile residuals, paired knickzones, and tributary deflection along a coherent trend—not just a local edge. A blind or degraded structure may warp channels without a sharp fault scarp. Repeated fluvial response at an independent subsurface boundary could persist after surface traces disappear. Resistant lithologic contacts, transient incision, drainage capture, landslides, roads/ditches, DEM seams, and channel-extraction settings. New to this checkout; differs from a single scarp/ridge detector. Prior LiDAR/scarp experiments on sibling sites could overlap; registry/method audit is required. Qualitative upside: moderate. Cost: high. No numeric uplift projected. USGS describes 3DEP products as free of charge and without use restrictions. The 3DEP 1 m catalog and availability/product details are public. The exact GEMS footprint coverage was not checked; not viable until tiles and lineage are verified.
3 · Paleothermal-witness alignment INGENIOUS paleogeothermal sinter/tufa features with GeoDAWN magnetic/gravity products and/or competition conductivity/strain. Test whether deposits form a coherent trend that continues beyond mapped Quaternary traces and follows an independent subsurface boundary. Old hot-spring deposits can record former discharge pathways; a continuing trend may reveal a structure not present in the modern fault catalogue. A USGS project record includes sinter and geophysical evidence for a concealed system; it does not validate this fault detector. Non-geothermal lake-margin tufa, sedimentary contacts, volcanic clustering, preservation/exposure bias, or exploration-history clustering. New to this checkout; point-witness-to-structure rather than a pure raster edge. GEMSDOE24/36 discuss geothermal approaches; sibling-network novelty is unverified. Qualitative upside: lower but potentially high precision. Cost: low–medium. No numeric uplift projected. Public GDR 1391 lists paleogeothermal features. Attributes, coordinate reference, duplicate points, licensing, and exact footprint overlap must be checked before testing.

Why no candidate was implemented

The lane paragraph is missing, and the required cache, labels, shared `evaluate_holdout.py`, shared `submission_writer.py`, frozen same-evaluator incumbent, and complete registry are absent. The official competition data tab requires login here. Implementing a private substitute would violate the reuse protocol; scoring an unbuffered or catalogue-leaking split would not validate the intended hypothesis.

Source listing and public availability do not establish spatial overlap or scientific value. No new external dataset was downloaded. No holdout was run, and no submission slot was used.

Pre-registration required before any experiment

  1. Freeze the single assigned lane, exact source versions/checksums, preprocessing, named mimic, hypotheses, controls, spatial blocks, whole-segment buffer, and stop thresholds before fitting.
  2. Derive every catalogue-based feature only from visible faults in each fold; mask the visible fault raster pixel-exactly.
  3. Run each feature alone as a leakage canary. Stop if AUC exceeds the required threshold until leakage is ruled out.
  4. Use the shared evaluator and writer. Report pooled HOLDOUT-DTI, evaluator version, withheld-positive count, and 95% CI—not a projection.
  5. Check the pre-placement surface and final dots against every pinned registry raster; any threshold violation is a duplicate stop.
  6. Respect the maximum of three experiments or two hours. A separate selector must authorize any real slot.
