## 4. Why the holdout and the live evidence disagree (new irregularity IR-K3-12)

Hide-and-recover withholds catalogue components. Many of those components are fragments of the
same trace, separated by a 1–5 px gap from a visible fragment. Recovering a fragment by
interpolation is easy. That explains why visible-catalogue distance features dominate (single
AUCs 0.77–0.79) and why E1 roughly doubles E3.

The live hidden set is different: expert-identified *new* faults that are not in the USGS
database. The live ledger shows those are depleted near catalogued traces. So the holdout rewards
exactly the behaviour that live scoring penalises. **Catalogue-geometry learners need a
fragment-censored holdout before they can be trusted.** Concretely: withhold only components more
than 5 px from every visible component, or score with truth within 5 px of visible censored on both
the TP and FN sides.

This strengthens the earlier finding that catalogue holdouts do not predict live scores. It also
names the mechanism.

## 5. Why GEMSDOE32 `h33-h33-2-b2` scored 0.2778 (OWNER-REPORTED; no organizer receipt)

- **Lineage (saved bytes, exact nesting).** H19-5 solid network, 121,131 px (0.1922) ⊃ D2.8 Poisson dots, 44,090 (0.2600) ⊃ B1-pruned, 40,199 (0.2708) ⊃ **B2-pruned, 37,654 (0.2778)**.
- **Dot spacing.** Thinning to 2.8 px (about 280 m, just under the 300 m kernel radius) keeps nearly all of the kernel's TP coverage on the network. It also cuts emitted pixels, and so FP mass, by 64% (121,131 → 44,090). DTI weights FP at 0.2 and FN at 0.8, so this trade wins.
- **Pruning near the catalogue.** Removing dots 0–1 px and 1–2 px from catalogued faults gained 2.8e-6 and 2.75e-6 DTI per removed dot. Both equal the value of deleting a pure false positive (≈ 0.2·DTI/D). So the dots near the catalogue carried essentially zero true positives, consistent with a test set that excludes catalogued faults.
- **No exploitable registration offset.** Over 24,167 H19-5 pixels within 3 px of labels, the mean offset is (+0.075, +0.039) px. The registration test was negative.

**Can it be beaten?** All 12 measured files scoring ≥ 0.2560 sit ≥ 97.7% within 3 px of H19-5 (minimum: GEMSDOE36 PINN, 97.75%) (`evidence/uniqueness_frontier.json`). The best file *outside* that lane is GEMSDOE70 p5 at 0.2478, with 62% on H19-5.

A unique file can therefore beat 0.2778 only by finding off-network true positives at a rate above
the on-network rate, about 0.15 TP per dot. GEMSDOE70's off-network dots achieve about 0.1. Neither
CCL nor any lane in this repository has shown that.

The honest answer: not with current evidence. The most promising lever is H3/H4-type off-network
signals scored on a fragment-censored holdout, combined with the depletion-aware collar (H2).

## 7. Limitations

- The holdout is biased toward recovering catalogue fragments (§4), so HOLDOUT-DTI gains for catalogue-geometry features are inflated relative to live performance.
- The LLI is pessimistic for files that sit off the H19-5 network: it projects GEMSDOE70 at 0.138 against an owner-reported 0.2478. Its CCL projection is a go/no-go signal, not an estimate.
- B=5 for the live file was not holdout-validated.
- The archived current best (SMGC) could not be regenerated, so its comparison is unpaired.
- The registry is a snapshot (2026-10-28 HEADs) of the listed owners only.
- Organizer format acceptance of zeros-outside files is unconfirmed.
