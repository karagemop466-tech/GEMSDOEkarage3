How to Submit to the Competition

  1. Download the file: Click one of the download links at the top of this page. Recommended: the -nan.tif (NaN outside footprint, matches the official "null or nan outside bounds" rule) or its .zip wrapper.
  2. Verify locally (optional): Run python scripts/check_format.py <file> --template docs/downloads/sample_submission.tif to confirm the file passes the format gate before uploading.
  3. Go to DrivenData: Open the GEMS competition page, sign in, and navigate to the submission form.
  4. Upload the file: Select the single-band GeoTIFF (.tif) or a .zip containing exactly one .tif. The file must match the competition's CRS (EPSG:32611), shape (3730×3292), and geotransform.
  5. Enter a note: Use a unique submission name and a short note (≤140 characters) to identify your submission, e.g. "karage3-blind-conduit-stepper-v1: off-catalogue stepover/relay conduit targeting, unique vs 9 focus sites".
  6. Save the receipt: Record the organizer's submission receipt (file hash + score) against your local file for auditability.

Geological Hypothesis — Why This Approach

Hypothesis: Blind stepover/relay ramp conduits host geothermal vents missing from the catalogue

  • Layers: USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue faults (labels.tif), derived distance transform.
  • Physical signature: Intermediate-distance zones (0.5–3 km) from known faults — far enough to be off-catalogue ("blind"), close enough to be structurally linked to the same fault system.
  • Why it catches missing faults: All nine focus submissions cluster on or immediately adjacent to catalogue traces. Off-catalogue stepover zones are systematically under-sampled by the dotted-ridge family.
  • How it differs: Continuous surface (not binary dots), targets distance-decay sweet spot at ~1.5 km from catalogue faults, places zero mass on catalogue traces, uses Gaussian-weighted stepover centers rather than uniform dotting.
