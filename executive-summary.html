Why GEMSDOE32 (0.2778) led the focus sites

GEMSDOE32's h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros (0.2778, user-reported/unverified) is not a new discovery. It is the same dotted-ridge dot family as GEMSDOE24 (0.2477), GEMSDOE25 (0.2600), GEMSDOE31 (0.2708), and GEMSDOE36 (0.2750), with progressive pruning of dots within 2 px of known USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue faults:

  • GEMSDOE24 d1.5 → GEMSDOE25 d2.8: 60,069 → 44,090 dots, same dots at wider spacing
  • GEMSDOE25 d2.8 → GEMSDOE31 d2.8: 44,090 → 40,199 dots; 3,891 dots removed, all within d ≤ 2.0 px of catalogue traces
  • GEMSDOE31 → GEMSDOE32 d2.8: 40,199 → 37,654 dots; 2,545 more dots removed, all within d ≤ 2.0 px of known faults

Why pruning raises DTI: The metric (α=0.2, β=0.8) penalizes false positives at weight 0.2 per kernel unit. Dots on known faults are masked to zero — they yield no true-positive credit but consume budget. Removing 2,545 redundant collar dots cuts the α-weighted FP denominator by ~509 units at near-zero TP loss: 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778.

Can we beat 0.2778? Yes — two levers: (1) prune harder (remove all dots within 3 px of visible faults), (2) add off-catalogue coverage at geothermal surface expressions (hot springs >60°C, vents, conductivity, potential-field anomalies). 1,535 hot springs (>60°C) lie >500 m from any mapped fault (GDR #1391). Our H-GEO-CONDUIT combines both: Holdout-DTI 0.4239.
