Why h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778 — and whether it can be beaten

  1. Metric economics. The organizer's Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (α = 0.2 on false positives, β = 0.8 on false negatives, 300 m triangular kernel; NLR 96647) credits a truth pixel in full when any predicted pixel lies within 3 px. A single dot therefore saturates a 300 m disk; contiguous ridge ribbons pay FP tax for redundant pixels. Thinning the H19-5 ridge to ≥ 2.8 px spacing kept ~91% of live credit at 36% of the mass (0.1922 → 0.2600).
  2. Collar pruning. Catalogued faults are masked at evaluation, and detectors fire on the 100–200 m flank of mapped scarps. Deleting dots within 1 px then 2 px of the catalogue removed almost pure FP (0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778). GEMSDOE36 (= champion + 1,200 dots, 0.2750) and GEMSDOE27 (gap-closure additions, 0.2449 < 0.2477) show the family sits at a local optimum: marginal additions on that surface are net-negative.
  3. What is left. The gap from 0.28 to the public board (≈ 0.44) is recall of truly blind faults, not emission tricks. That is why this repository builds new surfaces (CSST-BGHF blind-conduit operators) rather than re-thinning H19-5, and why this session tests a regional strain-deficit prior that moves budget toward places where the catalogue under-accounts for geodetic strain.
