Executive guide / read before uploading

From evidence to submission.

The competition accepts a georeferenced prediction raster. A valid file is necessary, but it is not the same as a strong, unique, or authorized submission.

When a candidate is actually ready

  1. Join and check eligibility. Register and enroll in the GEMS competition. The official rules govern eligibility, data rights, final deliverables, and required disclosure of generative-AI use. This project cannot sign in or accept terms for you.
  2. Check the research result, not a forecast. The run card must show the assigned method lane, each feature's leakage canary, and pooled whole-segment buffered HOLDOUT-DTI with evaluator version, withheld positives, 95% confidence interval, and a paired improvement against the same-evaluator current best. A holdout is not an organizer-score estimate.
  3. Check both uniqueness stages. The pre-placement surface and final dots must be tested against the same complete, current registry. Correlation above 0.90 or more than 70% of dots within three pixels of any registered raster is a stop. The pinned lattice currently obstructs the usual 37,654-dot budget; see the proof.
  4. Validate the actual saved bytes. Use the shared template writer, then reopen the exact TIFF. Check one float32 band, the organizer template’s CRS, shape and geotransform, no masked/non-finite values inside the footprint, and values in [0,1]. Reject external mask sidecars whose bytes are not covered by the TIFF digest. Our read-only validator checks these independently; it cannot establish the template’s authenticity or organizer acceptance. The current shared writer and evaluator silently binarize probabilities; the open template issue blocks continuous outputs until repaired upstream.
  5. Resolve the outside-footprint encoding. The published format says data outside the bounds should be null/NaN. A prior upload reported a [0,1] error, but the rejected file and receipt are unavailable. The validator reports null-outside and all-finite checks separately; neither is represented as proven portal acceptance. Do not silently clip or replace values to hide a model error.
  6. Use the separate weekly-slot selector. The rules PDF says up to three feedback submissions per week and one final selection for both prize rounds. Actual remaining quota and selector approval require the signed-in submission page. A passed automated research gate alone does not authorize an upload.
  7. Download, then upload only an approved file. When a future candidate passes every gate and is separately selected, its exact one-click TIFF, SHA-256, unique name and note of at most 140 characters belong at the top of the overview. On DrivenData choose “Submit” → “Make new submission,” select the single-band TIFF (or a ZIP containing one TIFF), paste the note, and submit. Save the organizer receipt together with the exact file SHA-256. No upload is performed by this project today.
Deadline discrepancy. The competition homepage lists 3 December 2026, 23:59 UTC; Appendix A.1 of the rules PDF says 5:00 p.m. ET on the deadline date. Confirm the applicable deadline with the organizer and plan conservatively; do not assume those times are interchangeable.

Why the previous range error is unresolved

A negative finite nodata sentinel, an out-of-range pixel, or non-finite values could cause a failure depending on how the portal reads the file. Without the rejected TIFF and organizer response, the precise cause is unknown. The validator explicitly tests finite sentinels even when a raster mask hides them; it does not mutate the raster or claim a successful upload.
