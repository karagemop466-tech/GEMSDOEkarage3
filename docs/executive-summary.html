PhD-level analysis

Why GEMSDOE32 (0.2778) led — and how a new submission could beat it

GEMSDOE32's h33-h33-2-b2 (reported 0.2778) is a pruned subset of the GEMSDOE31 dotted-h19-5 family: it removed 2,545 dots within 200 m of existing catalogue faults. Under the distance-weighted Tversky index (α=0.2 false-positive penalty, β=0.8 hidden-truth reward), removing redundant collar dots cuts the FP penalty at near-zero true-positive loss (0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 user-reported).

1

Prune dots near known faults

GEMSDOE32 dropped dots within 200 m of existing_faults.tif. Those dots were largely false positives near catalogue traces but not at hidden-truth locations, so removing them lifted the score.

2

Target off-catalogue faults

Hidden geothermal vents are not all in the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. Dots placed away from known traces target faults missing from the catalogue and have higher true-positive yield per emitted pixel.

3

Why the new file is unique

The new karage3-novel-faulthalo surface combines conductivity-gradient, basement-depth-gradient, gradient straightness, dilation rate, radiometric total count and 1 m LiDAR scarp — a different physical basis than dotted-h19-5 topography/multiline corroboration, with dots placed 0-1 px off the fault trace rather than on it. Verified: 9.09% / 3.84% within-3px overlap vs GEMSDOE32/GEMSDOE25, rank correlation ~0, 0% exact pixel intersection.

Build & validation

How the new unique TIF was built and validated

Hypothesis — conductive-basement thermal-structural co-location

Along catalogue fault strands, geothermal upflow localises where independent physical fields co-vary: subsurface conductive/basement boundary (cond_surf & depth_to_base_surf gradient), active extension (geod_dilaterate), radiometric alteration (total count) and 1 m LiDAR scarp expression. Dots sit on the top-N co-located hotspots in the 0–1 px halo off the fault trace.

Named non-fault mimic: lithologic/alteration contacts and erosion scarps can produce coincident conductivity–dilation–scarp anomalies without a through-going fault.
