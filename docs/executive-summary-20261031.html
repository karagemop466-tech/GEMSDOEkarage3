2. Answer: why did GEMSDOE32 (0.2778) score highest, and can we beat it?

Verified facts (re-run today):

  • GEMSDOE32 is the most catalogue-avoiding of the nine: 5.77% of its dots lie within 3 px of the catalogue, against 19.5–19.9% for GEMSDOE24/25/27/30 (the four highest-catalogue files).
  • Its holdout DTI (catalogue withheld) is 0.0045, below budget-matched random placement (0.030–0.033). Its off-catalogue SGMC-calibrated DTI is 0.0641, the second highest of the nine (GEMSDOE36 is 0.0644).
  • Across the nine, reported score ranks with SGMC-cal (Spearman ρ = +0.950, saved in the run card) and against holdout DTI (ρ = -0.849). The catalogue-proximity correlation (ρ ≈ −0.98) is from an ad-hoc calculation not yet saved as a receipt. N = 9; these are correlations, not causes.

Interpretation (judgement, not proven): the competition's test faults are "not contained within the current public USGS database". A file that keeps its dots off the catalogue while still covering off-catalogue structure should score better on the organizer's set, even though it is penalised by a catalogue-withholding holdout. That matches the ordering above. This is why the catalogue holdout and the leaderboard disagree.

Can we beat 0.2778? Not with anything tested today. The off-catalogue bar is SGMC-cal > 0.0644. The random placement at 37,654 dots scores 0.047–0.049 on that measure, so the nine-site family sits only about 0.015 above random. Our three new candidates did not reach the bar (table 3).
