## 8. Answers to the three questions

**Why did GEMSDOE32 `h33-h33-2-b2-…-zeros` score 0.2778 (USER-REPORTED, unverified)?**
The repo's own analysis (`registry/irregularities.json`, IR-K3-09 and `knowledge/irregularities.md`) says that the mask was built with the full USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. Every dot is more than 2 px from any catalogue pixel. The competition's test labels are non-catalogue faults, so a mask that avoids the catalogue is placed where the target faults are likely to be. Its 0.2778 is therefore a property of the **construction**, not evidence of a general detector. Under the catalogue-holdout protocol the same mask scores 0.0045 because the holdout withholds the very faults the mask avoids. The honest fold-local estimate of its rule is 0.0992 [0.0815, 0.1204]. These figures are HOLDOUT-DTI from the repo's evaluator (`GEMSDOE32-HOLDOUT-v3-visible-only`, 22,232 withheld positives). They are not organizer numbers.

**Can we beat 0.2778?**
Not verifiably from this sandbox. No local proxy tracks the leaderboard. Across 18 registry rasters with user-reported scores, the catalogue-holdout proxy has Spearman ρ = −0.55 (p = 0.018), and the off-catalogue proxy has ρ = 0.08 (p = 0.75) (`evidence/proxy-calibration-20261029.json`). A candidate that wins the holdout may still lose on the leaderboard. The only receipt that counts is a real submission, and the protocol forbids spending a weekly slot without a holdout win.

**Is a unique TIFF possible?**
Under option (a) in §6, no. Under (b) or (c), a new candidate would have to be built and measured afresh. The earlier candidates that failed uniqueness are listed in §4.
