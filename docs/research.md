## What the task actually measures

The [official problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) asks for **fault** predictions. Faults can form fluid pathways, but a predicted fault is not evidence of an active geothermal vent, reservoir temperature, commercial resource, or confirmed discovery. Existing mapped faults are incomplete; experts supplied additional unseen faults for evaluation. The public leaderboard is only one split; the selected final raster is evaluated against the private initial-round labels and then against an expanded expert-reviewed set. A catalogue hide-and-recover proxy is useful for comparing methods but is not known to be calibrated to that unseen target.

The official metric computes a distance-weighted Tversky index with α=0.2, β=0.8 and a 300 m triangular kernel on 100 m pixels. Each ground-truth pixel receives its maximum nearby prediction credit; each positive prediction contributes distance-weighted false-positive mass. Consequently, nearby predictions may be redundant, but thinning can also remove real recall. Geometry alone cannot establish an optimum, causation, or the hidden score. [Official formula and submission format](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/#performance-metric).

## Literal lattice stop: a reproducible counting bound

Within the mirrored footprint, the registered lattice has 206,895 positive cells and its closed three-pixel Euclidean neighborhood covers 5,160,724 of 5,167,373 cells, leaving **U = 6,649** uncovered. If an in-footprint binary candidate has **B** distinct dots, at most U of those dots can lie outside the covered set. Therefore its directional overlap is at least `max(0, B-U)/B`. At **B = 37,654**, at least **31,005/37,654 = 82.34%** must overlap—greater than the protocol's 70% stop. This result is independent of the geological model and does not depend on a complete registry.

For B ≤22,163 this one counting bound does not rule out a pass; it does **not** demonstrate that such a candidate exists or scores well, and it says nothing about pre-placement surface correlations. Do not concentrate dots into the few remaining holes merely to defeat the rule. Resolving whether a calibration lattice belongs in the literal any-raster registry is an explicit protocol/selector decision, not an automatic exemption. The [pinned shared registry](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE32/blob/73485f27cf230af770167397c2b72cb9ce3b3f25/registry/focus_registry_manifest.json) includes the same lattice digest. No full contemporary registry was fetched or compared in this run.
