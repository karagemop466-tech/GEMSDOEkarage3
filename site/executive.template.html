How submission works, once gates pass

  1. Generate and validate the assigned lane. Use the shared fold-local holdout and writer, test every feature alone for leakage, compare against the same-evaluator best, and check the full current registry before and after placement. Any missing or failed gate means stop.
  2. Run the separate slot selector. Only a candidate with a recorded positive selector decision, exact-file hash and available weekly quota can become a download. This project has no such candidate now. A file’s local format check alone is not authorization.
  3. Use the official submission page. After approval, open DrivenData GEMS, sign in and use its submission flow to select the approved single-band GeoTIFF. The logged-in interface was not accessible during this review.
  4. Enter the associated note and save the receipt. Use the candidate’s unique release name and its note (at most 140 characters), then record the organizer’s result against the exact file hash. The official rules describe a limit of three feedback submissions per week, subject to current site limits; the account’s remaining quota is unknown here. They also require narrative disclosure of generative-AI use and reproducible code and documentation from finalists.
  5. Select one final submission. The same chosen submission is used in both prize rounds. Public feedback is not the private or final score. Do not spend a slot merely because a file is downloadable.

The format and the range-error caveat

The official problem description requires one float32 layer in the training grid’s CRS, resolution and bounds, with confidence values in [0, 1] and null/NaN outside bounds. The submit form additionally requires the sample’s shape and geotransform.

Our validator reopens the exact bytes and checks every in-footprint value, raster masks, datatype, CRS, shape and transform against the supplied template. It reports both a conservative null-outside-template-footprint interpretation and a whole-array-finite [0, 1] diagnostic. On a template with an irregular footprint, those conventions can differ. Neither proves portal acceptance or identifies the cause of your previous range error. Never silently clip invalid inputs.

A deterministic stop, not a score

The pinned lattice has 206,895 positive cells. On the pinned mirror footprint, 6,649 cells lie farther than 3 pixels from it. For any set of N distinct in-footprint dots, at least max(0, N − 6,649) must be within the lattice neighborhood. At N = 37,654, that is at least 31,005 / 37,654 = 82.34%, exceeding the strict 70% rule regardless of placement.

This is a one-witness geometric lower bound—not a similarity of geological mechanisms and not a universal impossibility proof for every smaller budget. Do not exploit the remaining holes to manufacture uniqueness. The rule still includes the lattice until the protocol changes.

Why might GEMSDOE32 have led?

In our independent pixel audit, the GEMSDOE32 H33-B2 support is a subset of the GEMSDOE31 historical support: 2,545 dots near the mirrored known-fault labels were removed. GEMSDOE36’s historical support contains H33-B2 plus 1,200 dots; GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 are identical inside the footprint.

The official distance-weighted Tversky metric gives each truth pixel its maximum nearby prediction credit, while predicted mass away from hidden truth contributes false-positive penalty. Thus thinning and pruning may reduce redundant or low-yield mass. This is a plausible mechanism—not proof of the score’s cause. Hidden labels and artifact-linked receipts are unavailable, and higher performance cannot be promised.
